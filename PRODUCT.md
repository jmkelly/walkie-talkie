# Walkie Talkie — product

**Point of view.** Walkie Talkie is a shared two-way channel that behaves like one piece of hardware: one room, one transmit key, one running log, and no feed to manage.

**Design read.** A single-purpose realtime console for a parent and a kid, often one on a desktop and one on a phone. It should read at a glance: who is in the channel, whether the mic is open, and what was just said. The visual language is a warm graphite radio console — bone-white type, one signal-orange accent, stenciled micro-labels, a monospace readout, speaker-grille texture, and a transmit key that looks pressable.

The messaging thread is still the primary surface. The console chrome exists to make the channel state and the transmit key unmistakable around it.

## References, one principle each

| Reference | Principle | How it shows up here (not a copy of the surface) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teenage Engineering OP-1 | **Printed controls.** A control and its readout are one object; labels sit at the control, not in a manual. | The Talk key carries its own mic icon and label, with the channel readout above it and the hint directly beneath it. |
| Nintendo Switch | **Side-coded.** Local and remote have fixed sides, so the eye learns the layout without relying on color alone. | Your messages sit right with the accent tint; the other person's sit left on graphite. The meters are labeled You / Them. |
| Braun T 1000 receiver | **Functional texture.** The grille and dial are the decoration; nothing is ornament for its own sake. | The speaker-grille dot field appears in the lower status rail and faintly behind the page; the empty log carries the station mark. The meters are segmented LEDs. |
| Linear | **Quiet chrome.** Hairline seams and weight carry hierarchy; the accent is reserved for state and action. | The shell is one frame with seams, not a stack of floating cards. Orange appears on the live lamp, the open mic, and focus. |
| Discord | **Presence first.** Who is here and whether the mic is open is visible before the conversation. | The channel lamp and status line stay visible in every state, including live. |
| Playdate | **One memorable mark.** A product silhouette that survives at favicon size. | The station mark is a handheld radio with an antenna and side key, used in the header, favicon, and empty log. |

**Cover the logo.** Hide the wordmark and the mark. The split console, channel readout, segmented meters, and pressed-metal Talk key still identify the product. If they do not, the layout is not doing its job.

**The one job.** Know the channel is open and say something in one click. Chat is the log of that channel, not a separate destination.

**Not this.** No avatars, no presence bubbles beyond the channel lamp, no emoji, no gamer neon, no second accent, no card grid, no onboarding tour.
